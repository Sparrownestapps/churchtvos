# Church TV OS is live on Fire TV, Google TV and Roku

Picture a Sunday at about ten to nine. Someone is behind the nursery TV, hunting for the right cable. The countdown video is on a laptop. The laptop is in the car. Forty minutes later, the room is fine, because rooms always are, but a good volunteer has spent the best part of her morning being a technician.

I've lived some version of that Sunday more than once. I'm a worship pastor in Cambridge, Ontario, and I lead the tech team at Forward Church. When we wanted the kids rooms to run on their own screens instead of laptops and USB sticks, I couldn't find anything that did what we needed on the cheap, common hardware we already had. So I started building. [CONFIRM details of the Forward Church story before publishing.]

Today that thing has a name, **Church TV OS**, and it's live for every church.

## What it does

Church TV OS puts your kids-room curriculum, your lobby announcements and your Sunday slides on the TVs you already have. You pair a TV with a six-character code, build and schedule what you want from the web, and run the room from your phone.

It's one system with three modules, and a single TV can switch between them on a schedule.

### Kids
Playlists of videos, countdowns, song lyrics and YouTube, scheduled per room, with a weekly rotation so Week 2 is ready when Week 2 comes. The TV shows what's coming and waits for a leader to press Play, so the person in the room is always in charge.

### Signage
Pictures, video and text signs that loop on lobby and hallway screens on a weekly schedule, with nobody standing at the TV. Landscape or portrait. When nothing is scheduled the screen goes black instead of showing last month's announcement.

### Slides
Decks for your services. Text, songs with lyrics, pictures, video, countdowns and live streams, in themes and backgrounds that look like your church. Import a PDF or PowerPoint you already have. Step through from the TV remote or your phone. It isn't trying to replace the production rig in a big sanctuary. It's for the rooms and the churches that don't have one: a chapel, an overflow room, a satellite, the youth room.

Put those together and one TV earns its keep all week: Signage Monday to Saturday, Kids on Sunday morning.

## Set it Thursday, forget it Sunday

The TV downloads what's scheduled ahead of time, so if the internet drops on Sunday it keeps playing from saved content and catches up when the connection returns. Volunteers get the operator role: Next, Back, Blank, Play now and a message to the screen. That role can't edit anything, so nobody can break the room while forty kids are watching.

## Which TVs?

Live today: **Fire TV, Google TV (and Android TV), and Roku.** LG and Samsung are coming soon.

A few honest notes, because I'd want them if I were you:

* Newer Fire TV sticks that run Amazon's Vega OS can't run Church TV OS. Other Fire TV devices and Google TV boxes can.
* The Roku channel is new. It's the youngest of the three, and some things differ (for example YouTube items aren't supported on Roku). I'd love your feedback if you try it.
* The church is responsible for the licences for the lyrics, music, video and curriculum it uploads. Church TV OS plays what you give it. It doesn't supply copyrighted content.
